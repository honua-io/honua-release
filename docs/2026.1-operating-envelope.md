# Honua 2026.1 supported operating envelope

Use the [release notes](../release-notes/2026.1.md), signed release lock and
exact-candidate receipts to determine the qualified deployment and component set.
This page defines operating boundaries; a scope declaration, implementation PR,
configured limit or historical measurement does not establish qualification.
**Not qualified in 2026.1** means no supported operating claim is established,
never unlimited capacity.

For the first lock-bearing release, rollback certification must retain and
exercise its exact attested candidate lock and explicitly record that no earlier
lock exists. That lock becomes the ordinary rollback target for the next release.

## 2026.1 rc.2 deployment certification scope

The 2026-11-09 founder ruling supersedes the deployment tiers below for rc.2. The sole
production target is **AWS ECS Fargate + RDS PostGIS + ElastiCache + Console in
S3/CloudFront**, deployed with the `honua-iac` ECS stack. Both `aws-ecs/redis-off` and
`aws-ecs/redis-on` remain required evidence cells. A scope declaration or static stack
check is not live qualification evidence.

**Lambda and Kubernetes/Helm are not certified in 2026.1 rc.2; ECS is the supported
production deployment.** Docker Compose remains the local evaluation quickstart and
is not a certified production target. Earlier measurements and future GA obligations
below do not expand rc.2 certification.

## 2026.1 tiers (founder re-tier, 2026-10-25)

The [2026-10-25 founder re-tier](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-release/issues/376#issuecomment-6407009748) sets the 2026.1 tiers and supersedes earlier tier rulings where they differ:

- **GA:** Docker Compose on-prem and AWS ECS. The two `aws-ecs` cells (Redis off and on) are the
  only required cloud cells.
- **Preview:** AWS Lambda, Helm (EKS and other Kubernetes distributions), the GP catalog and Honua Studio. Preview
  surfaces carry honest labels and their security, isolation and integrity floors, not GA evidence.
  GP authorization and correctness fixes stay on the 2026.1 floor whatever the catalog's label.
- **Architecture:** x86_64 only. ARM64 children of multi-architecture images are not qualified.
- **Tenancy:** single-tenant only; multi-tenancy is internal (below).

## Multi-tenancy is internal (ruling R29, 2026-10-03)

Multi-tenancy is **internal**: Honua uses it only on its own demo infrastructure
(honua-demo-infra, `demo.honua.io`), and that demo stack is its only deployment. It is not offered
for customer deployment in 2026.1 and is not GA, Preview or Experimental. It carries no
availability, performance, durability or support claim and is not counted in any GA denominator.
No customer-facing multi-tenant claim exists: the Fair Core License (FCL-1.0-ALv2) bars third
parties from offering Honua as a competing managed service. Every customer topology on this page is single-tenant. This
supersedes the 2026-09-04 Preview/trial wording ([release plan](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-release/issues/376)).

## Licensing (operator ruling, 2026-09-12)

2026.1 ships with licensing disabled (`Licensing__Mode=Disabled`): no license file, minting, edition gating or capacity metering. All catalog entitlements are active; serving-unit bands are neither measured nor enforced. Authentication, authorization, safety limits and release maturity remain in force. COG/Zarr/GeoParquet/PMTiles retain their GA scope; the GP catalog, alerting and offline sync are Preview; multi-tenancy is internal (ruling R29). Licensing hardening, bands, metering, marketplace and the Console licensing page move to 2026.2 (server#4720, iac#190, helm#78, console#384).

Install with the [2026.1 deployment settings](INSTALL-2026.1.md). Candidate gates require authenticated `GET /api/v1/admin/license` to report `data.mode: disabled`; health alone cannot prove this. This ruling supersedes the September 5 strict paid-license failure-mode contract for 2026.1. Qualified capacity and recovery still require their own receipts.

## Redis-off topology (owner decision 3, 2026-10-08; extended 2026-10-10)

Redis is a requirement of the governed control plane in 2026.1 (a Postgres proposal store is
2026.1.x). A Redis-off deployment is a serving, configure and style/render topology: governed
operations refuse up front with the typed `unavailable` refusal (`missingDependency: redis`) and the
capability manifest reports the capability `dependency-unavailable`. On a Redis-off cell the
terminal journey records those stages by the typed refusal instead of executing them: stages 5-8
(GP, durable compositions, proposal, approval) under decision 3, and stage 3 (publish-service,
which runs through the governed operation runtime) under its 2026-10-10 extension. Redis-on
deployments are unchanged. With no layer published, stage 4 (style/render) and stages 6-8 have no
subject on a Production Redis-off cell and stay blocked; that cell's journey is not yet qualified.
See the [journey rule](../certification/terminal-journey/README.md).

On Redis-off, direct `service.publish` by an admin-role caller executes without
the proposal store and reports `durable: false`. Studio publication requests,
`honua_admin_*` tools, apply-style and every proposal path retain the typed
refusal. Exact-candidate receipts must prove the applicable path; older runs or
merged implementation changes do not qualify it.

## Operation policy in Production (owner decision 10, 2026-10-10)

`ASPNETCORE_ENVIRONMENT=Production` enables `Operations:Policy` with a fail-closed `Deny` default.
The production policy evaluates operator rules first, then built-in rules that allow `admin`-role callers and
`scoped-admin-key` callers, then `Deny`. `admin:write` and `admin:manage` keys resolve to the full
`admin` role. Operators opt out with `Operations:Policy:BuiltInProductionRules=false`; the startup
validator keeps the fail-closed default. Approved-operation keys bypass the typed policy decision
point through their approved proposal id.

Deployments may supply explicit `operations_policy_rules`; the AWS modules accept
`Allow`, `RequireApproval`, `DryRunFirst` and `Deny`. Record the effective rules
with the deployment evidence. Production also permits only administrators to
author Studio content until `Studio:EndUserAuthorization:Enabled` is set; the
module input is `studio_end_user_authorization`.

## Customer domains (owner instruction, 2026-10-11)

Honua runs on the customer's own FQDN. Nothing in the product may assume a `honua.io` host
(ruling `customer-domains-2026-10-11`):

- Configure `Public__BaseUrl` with the customer's HTTPS origin; AWS module
  deployments use `public_base_url` or the configured `domain_name`. Absolute
  publication links, `activeUrl` and capability links must use `Public:BaseUrl`,
  never the request host. A value without a scheme is ignored in favor of
  loopback, and admin OIDC login answers 503 outside Development without it.
- Configure allowed hosts and certificate/DNS ownership for that customer origin.
  A provider's own certification domain, demo smoke or CSP allowlist does not
  qualify a customer's zone or establish product domain requirements.
- Prove the actual candidate's login, generated links and capability metadata on
  the configured origin. Source changes or module defaults do not prove that
  deployment's behavior.

## 2. Qualified dataset sizes, request limits, GP concurrency and storage

Capacity qualification requires measured acceptance bounds and an independent
post-freeze receipt for the exact candidate. The
[frozen capacity file](../certification/capacity-envelope.v1.json) and
[capacity contract](CAPACITY-ENVELOPE-2026.1.md) define the envelope; threshold-shaped
fixtures, configured caps and older local-runner measurements do not prove
production capacity or cloud equivalence.

Operator ruling A (2026-09-13) excludes `activeSubscriptions` and `alertEvaluationsPerSecond` from the 2026.1 capacity envelope. Realtime subscriptions and customer alerting are **Preview**; Preview features carry no capacity promise. Founder ruling `gp-out-of-ga-capacity-envelope-2026-10-27` makes `gpWorkers` and `gpQueueDepth` Preview evidence and takes GPServer requests out of the GA SLO population (GP is Preview and GP capacity is not qualified in 2026.1), so the six GA dimensions are `tenants`, `services`, `layersPerService`, `featuresPerLayer`, `maximumFeaturePayloadBytes`, and `concurrentVirtualUsers`. The eight required SLO signals remain availability, error rate, p95/p99 latency, throughput, queue age, saturation, and recovery. Founder ruling `capacity-steady-state-and-drill-2026-10-27` computes the steady-state signals only over the measured window, which excludes the fault-injection period, and judges the fault drill, which runs after that window, on its own: every worker and Redis fault recovers within 19 s and every Postgres fault within 36 s (ruling `capacity-drill-per-fault-bounds-2026-10-27`), with no data loss and no stuck jobs. See [the capacity ruling](CAPACITY-ENVELOPE-2026.1.md#scope).

## 3. What happens when a limit is reached

Configured controls and receipt-backed qualification are different facts. There
is no candidate-bound, topology-specific overload/recovery receipt here, so
graceful behavior at the complete operating envelope is **not qualified in
2026.1**. Use the protocol error returned by the supported SDK/CLI/MCP path;
do not assume every rejection is an HTTP rate-limit response or that a timeout
proves a mutation did not run.

| Limit or failure boundary | Current behavior and customer consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Request body with declared length above the configured cap | The pinned [limits middleware](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/blob/4ca8326f37b3225315033ee58e1a652e231992df/src/Honua.Server/Features/Infrastructure/Middleware/LimitsEnforcementMiddleware.cs) rejects before the handler with status **413**, message `Request payload exceeds maximum allowed size`, and the configured maximum in the detail. Its [protocol formatter](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/blob/4ca8326f37b3225315033ee58e1a652e231992df/src/Honua.Hosting/Features/Models/StandardErrorResponseFormatter.cs) selects the route's error envelope. This is a source-confirmed error contract, not a topology qualification receipt; tested payload ceiling: **not qualified in 2026.1**. |
| Cooperative handler observes the request deadline before response starts | The same middleware emits status **408**, `Request timeout`, with the configured duration in the detail. It cannot replace a response already started, and handlers must observe its deadline token. This conditional rejection contract is source-confirmed; complete topology/deadline qualification is **not qualified in 2026.1**. Maps is a known exception below. |
| OGC Processes admission evaluator refuses a submission | The pinned [process endpoint](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/blob/4ca8326f37b3225315033ee58e1a652e231992df/src/Honua.Protocols.OgcApi/Processes/ProcessEndpoints.cs) returns **503**, `Service unavailable`, the admission message, and `Retry-After`. Respect that delay through the supported client. This describes the rejection branch, not proof that admission is atomic or all GP transports share the same status; throughput/concurrency qualification: **not qualified in 2026.1**. |
| Map request deadline | **Undefined — tracked as [server#4284](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/issues/4284).** The documented end-to-end deadline is not propagated through the OGC Maps render path; connected work can continue beyond it. Database command timeouts do not establish a whole-request bound. Broken promise: the documented timeout control on a supported Maps workflow. |
| Vector-tile byte budget | **Undefined — tracked as [server#4287](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/issues/4287).** The advertised tile-size setting is not enforced as an output-byte cap; feature-count caps do not bound encoded attribute bytes. | GP admission under concurrent nodes/restart | **Undefined — tracked as [server#3853](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/issues/3853).** Admission reads active work separately from submission and rate buckets are process-local; do not rely on an atomic fleet-wide cap. | Storage exhaustion, output publication or durable-store loss | A complete graceful-rejection and recovery envelope is **not qualified in 2026.1**. Classify a newly discovered blocker against a broken security/isolation,
data-integrity or documented supported-workflow promise. A missing measurement
alone establishes an unqualified claim, not a product defect.

## 4. Backup procedure and recovery expectation

Follow the pinned [backup and restore runbook](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/blob/4ca8326f37b3225315033ee58e1a652e231992df/docs/guides/deploy/backup-and-restore.md)
for database and file handling, with the durable-substrate caveat below:

1. Inventory the deployment's durable state: PostgreSQL data/metadata, migration
   journal and transactional outbox; configured upload/attachment/output stores;
   Redis job queue, logs and workflow state where enabled. Record the exact image,
   configuration, backup identities and checksums. Keep secrets outside this record.
2. Before any backup capture, block new mutations and job submissions, drain
   in-flight requests/jobs to completion, then stop application replicas, workers,
   schedulers, outbox dispatchers and external writers across all nodes. Verify no
   writes remain to PostgreSQL, Redis or the file/object stores. If draining or
   quiescing fails, abort the backup; do not claim a consistent recovery set.
3. Keep that write boundary in place through the entire PostgreSQL `pg_dump`
   (custom format, tools matching the database major version) and the matching
   file-volume/object-store capture, including every enabled durable substrate
   from step 1. Bucket versioning/replication alone is not a consistent snapshot:
   record the exact object versions captured during this boundary. Verify readable
   archives, checksums and matching metadata/reference bytes before releasing the
   boundary and resuming writers. If an enabled substrate cannot be captured, the
   set is incomplete; see the Redis caveat below. Database WAL/PITR remains an
   infrastructure-owned option, not evidence of a tested Honua recovery objective.
4. Stop traffic and all writers again before restore, and keep them stopped until
   every matching store has been restored. Use `pg_restore` into a clean database
   and restore matching file/object state before starting the application. Ensure
   required extensions exist and select an image compatible with the restored
   migration journal. Startup may apply forward migrations.
5. Restart and verify readiness plus an actual supported-client catalog/feature
   journey. Check restored counts/checksums and referenced output bytes. For enabled
   workers, Redis and outbox, prove restart recovery through real runtime paths;
   a catalog browse alone does not establish job recovery or outbox delivery.

**Redis caveat:** every enabled durable job/workflow substrate belongs in the
recovery set. A runbook exclusion cannot establish complete platform recovery.
AOF/reconnection guidance concerns restart resilience; it does not prove
recovery after primary-state destruction. Complete Redis/output/outbox backup
and recovery needs real runtime evidence, not PostgreSQL job-shaped fixtures.

**Substrate ruling (operator ruling A, 2026-09-12).** The 2026.1 soak and DR envelope is the
**local-docker substrate** — the single-tenant local Docker install (PostGIS, Redis and a local
file-storage volume) whose durable inventory the candidate manifest's `disasterRecovery` block now
resolves explicitly. That substrate is the scope for full-platform recovery qualification,
not a recorded pass; **AWS qualification remains unqualified in 2026.1**, so no ECS, Lambda or other cloud
recovery claim follows from a local-docker receipt, and the cloud rows in this page's tables stand
unchanged. The producer is [`e2e/dr-drill/full_platform.py`](../e2e/dr-drill/README.md): one run
that, for every substrate the manifest declares enabled, writes durable state through a real
product surface, backs it up, destroys the primary state, restores into a verified-empty store,
restarts onto a different runtime instance, and re-reads identical state, with RPO and RTO measured
from those observations. `tools/validate_dr_receipt.py` recomputes the recovery window from the
receipt's own timestamps, so the measurement cannot be asserted. The rows below still read
**not qualified in 2026.1** because a receipt only qualifies this candidate once it is signed from
`refs/heads/trunk` and bound here; the mechanism existing is not the receipt existing.

| Recovery expectation | Receipt, topology and measured scope |
| --- | --- |
| Whole-platform restore, Redis job recovery, output bytes and post-restart outbox delivery | **Not qualified in 2026.1**. PostgreSQL job-shaped fixtures and checksums captured before restart do not prove real durable-job, output or outbox recovery. |
| ECS, HA/failover, PITR, regional disaster, large datasets, backup frequency/retention and recovery SLO | **Not qualified in 2026.1**. Select and rehearse these with the deployment's infrastructure owner; no runbook default is a measured objective. |
| Upgrade and rollback | **Not qualified in 2026.1** in the [lock-derived upgrade table](SDK-SERVER-COMPATIBILITY.md#upgrade-edges). Reinstalling an older application does not undo a database migration. If that application cannot read the migrated schema and no verified reverse migration exists, returning to it requires a verified pre-upgrade backup restore. |

Budget API instances, control-plane processes, workers, migrations and admin
access together against the actual RDS connection capacity. Per-process pools,
proxy limits and reserved concurrency must be qualified as one deployment;
a proxy existing or a run with no connection-slot errors does not establish a
safe concurrency ceiling or successful background-job execution.

The [cut checklist](RELEASE-CUT-CHECKLIST.md) defines candidate admission.
Missing, skipped, stale or wrong-candidate receipts fail closed. A scope ruling,
implementation PR or container mirror alone does not establish execution proof;
read contract versions from the real candidate.

Deployment preparation is explicitly unadmitted: resolving source, image, client
and configuration pins is not a candidate cut. Prepared data must not appear in
`refs/nightly-candidates` or the customer install manifest.

The following full GA validation contract does not expand rc.2 scope.
Candidate admission and GA promotion check different Lambda bill phases. The
explicit `candidate-precut` phase permits Lambda's `ga-target` / `pending` rows
only after newly executed, exact-input bill items 1 and 2 pass and the current
published limits satisfy item 5's pre-cut half. This does not mark Lambda
Supported. The default `--exact-candidate` validation remains the full GA gate:
`status: supported`, `qualification: passed`, and genuine full-bill evidence,
including the two-certified-lock upgrade/rollback requirement. EKS has no such
phase exception: both Redis modes require their full live deployment, serving,
terminal journey and teardown proof before candidate admission.

New deployment receipts use `honua.deployment-execution-inputs/v1`. Their producer
captures the prepared manifest, actual effective deployment configuration and
immutable producer identity before deployment. The identity binds source,
images, architecture, client artifacts, contracts, schemas, IaC, Helm, fixtures
and configuration; new fields are bound by default. Only explicitly enumerated
train outputs (release label and derived artifact versions, snapshot/cut time,
and generated ledger coordinates) are excluded. The raw receipt records that
capture's digest and actual run identity after execution and owned teardown.
Admission verifies the capture, actual assertions, current policy hash and
producer provenance. Old evidence cannot acquire this binding after the fact.

Legacy `qualificationReceipt` references retain their original strict meaning:
an HTTPS `url` and `candidateManifestDigest` bind the entire parsed platform
manifest as canonical UTF-8 JSON (sorted keys, compact separators, unescaped
Unicode). Any manifest change invalidates a legacy binding. For Lambda, the
legacy URL still names `lambda-preview-receipt.json`; a run page is not a receipt.
Neither identity validation nor an implementation PR substitutes for AWS proof.
No receipt reference is declared until that proof exists. Full GA qualification
remains pending until all required bill items are proven.

`e2e-cloud-aws.yml` copies the release-account Lambda manifest with `crane` and
exits non-zero while `awsLambdaEcrDigest` is `pending-ecr-mirror`, after a re-pin. That
bootstrap is not the certification lane. A cloud-parity pass does not discharge
this bill, and the cloud report keeps `lambdaGaQualification` at `pending`.
